# Presentation Narrative

## Problem
Interruptions can break task context. Some autistic people report difficulty stopping, changing, or initiating activities, while cognitive-flexibility research shows meaningful group-level differences with substantial heterogeneity.

## Design choice
We avoid designing for an autism severity band. We target a specific experience profile and make the player experience the problem through gameplay.

## Mechanic
FOCUS -> INTERRUPTION -> SAVE CONTEXT -> SWITCH -> RETURN -> RECOVER -> DEDUCE

Quick Pin is an optional external context cue, not a score or diagnosis.

## Why mystery
Investigation naturally asks the player to hold a question in mind, compare evidence, and return to unfinished reasoning. That makes context loss meaningful instead of artificial.

## What we built
A Coupler vertical slice in which the player verifies a maintenance claim across an interruption and then completes a deduction.

## What we are claiming
The design is research-informed and demonstrates a plausible support mechanic.

## What we are not claiming
- that all autistic people experience the same difficulty;
- that the prototype diagnoses autism;
- that Quick Pin is clinically validated;
- that a short class playtest demonstrates therapeutic benefit.

## Future expansion
Additional Void-eager mystery pieces can plug into the same validated loop without expanding the engine.
