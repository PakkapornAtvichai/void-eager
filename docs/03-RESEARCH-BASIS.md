# Research Basis

## Claim boundary

We are not designing for an autism "band." Autism is heterogeneous. The prototype targets a narrower experience profile: cognitive flexibility/task switching and difficulty stopping, changing, and resuming activities.

## Autism evidence

Lage, Smith & Lawson, *A meta-analysis of cognitive flexibility in autism spectrum disorder* (Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews, 2024). The meta-analysis included 59 studies, 2,122 autistic participants without intellectual disability and 2,036 neurotypical controls, ages 4–85. It reported a statistically significant small-to-moderate group difference in cognitive flexibility and substantial heterogeneity.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38104788/

Buckle et al., *No Way Out Except From External Intervention: First-Hand Accounts of Autistic Inertia* (Frontiers in Psychology, 2021). Focus groups with 32 autistic adults described difficulty starting, stopping, and changing activities, and discussed scaffolding/environmental prompting that could support action.

Source: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.631596/full

## Interruption/HCI evidence

Trafton et al., *Preparing to resume an interrupted task* (International Journal of Human-Computer Studies, 2003). The study tested an interruption lag and found evidence that preparation before interruption can facilitate later task resumption.

Source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1071581903000235

## Serious-game evidence

A 2024 systematic review examined serious games targeting executive functions including attention, working memory, cognitive flexibility, and inhibitory control in neurodiverse populations. It supports the plausibility of the format but does not justify a clinical efficacy claim for this prototype.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39693133/

## Design translation

Research supports the problem space. The game mechanic is our design hypothesis: external context cues such as Quick Pin may make resumption easier or more legible to the player. We will test usability and perceived helpfulness, not clinical treatment outcomes.
